## Cross-border claims are no longer exceptions

Global commerce has made cross-border claims part of ordinary risk management. A policy may be issued in one market, the insured may serve clients in another, and evidence may sit across several legal systems.

That reality changes how liability claims should be assessed. The merits still matter, but so do jurisdiction, evidence access, procedural timing, and the practical enforceability of any outcome.

> A strong defence can weaken quickly when evidence, forum and policy response are not aligned.

## Jurisdiction and evidence

The choice of jurisdiction influences where a case is heard, but it also affects the collection and treatment of evidence. Witness availability, document production, privilege rules, translation needs, and local procedural expectations can all shape the trajectory of a claim.

For insurers, this means early claims triage must look beyond the allegation itself. The team should ask where the relevant documents are located, which courts or tribunals may assert authority, and whether policy wording gives enough clarity for defence-cost handling.

## Enforcement realities

Even where a party obtains a favourable judgment or award, commercial success depends on enforceability. If assets are located elsewhere, recovery may require additional proceedings and further legal spend.

This is why contract review, dispute resolution planning, and insurance placement should not be treated as separate processes. They are part of one risk architecture.

## Key takeaways

Cross-border claims require a disciplined map of forum, governing law, evidence, enforcement, and policy response. The earlier that map is created, the more control the insured and its advisers retain when a dispute emerges.
