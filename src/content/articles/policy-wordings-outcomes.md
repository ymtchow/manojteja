## Language is the architecture of coverage

Insurance outcomes often turn on words that looked ordinary at placement. A definition, exclusion, condition precedent, notification trigger, territorial clause, or jurisdictional limitation can become decisive once a claim arrives.

For senior insurance readers, the point is not that wordings should be longer. The point is that wordings should be more deliberate.

## Where wording issues appear

Common pressure points include definitions of claim, insured services, professional services, wrongful act, occurrence, territory, jurisdiction, defence costs, sanctions, retroactive dates, and aggregation.

In cross-border programmes, these terms become even more important because the policy may need to respond to facts, proceedings, and legal standards across multiple environments.

## Commercial drafting discipline

Strong wording review connects the insured's real operations with the policy's legal architecture. That means reviewing contracts, service territories, client profile, claims history, and dispute clauses before renewal terms are treated as final.

The best wordings are not merely insurer-friendly or insured-friendly. They are clear enough to reduce uncertainty when both parties most need certainty.
